Overstated

Do you just have to put up with hunger when dieting?

Short answer

Some hunger comes with a deficit, but how much is very changeable. Protein at meals, high-volume low-calorie foods, fewer ultra-processed foods and enough sleep each have trial evidence behind them. If you're starving all day, change the setup before you rely on willpower.

Protein fills you up, at least meal to meal

Systematic review and meta-analysis
Effect of short- and long-term protein consumption on appetite and appetite-regulating gastrointestinal hormones, a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials
Kohanmoo A, Faghih S, Akhlaghi M, Physiology & Behavior, 2020
49
acute trials
19
long-term trials

Read it on PubMed →

At the same calories, higher-protein meals reduced hunger and increased fullness, and moved appetite hormones the right way. That held for single meals. The long-term trials didn't show a clear effect. So protein helps you get through the day; whether it makes the whole diet easier is less certain.

Eat more food, not more calories

Both groups cut fat. One group was also told to eat more water-rich foods, mostly fruit and vegetables. With no calorie targets, that group ate a greater weight of food, lost more weight, and reported less hunger.

Ultra-Processed Diets Cause Excess Calorie Intake and Weight Gain: An Inpatient Randomized Controlled Trial of Ad Libitum Food Intake
Hall KD, Ayuketah A, Brychta R et al., Cell Metabolism, 2019
20
adults
+508

Read it on PubMed →

Living in a research ward and eating as much as they wanted, people ate about 500 kcal a day more on an ultra-processed diet than on an unprocessed one, even though the meals offered were matched for calories, sugar, fat, fibre and salt. They gained 0.9 kg in two weeks on one and lost 0.9 kg on the other.

Sleep is part of the diet

Randomised crossover trial
Insufficient sleep undermines dietary efforts to reduce adiposity
Nedeltcheva AV, Kilkus JM, Imperial J et al., Annals of Internal Medicine, 2010
10
adults
1.4 vs 0.6 kg
fat lost

Read it on PubMed →

The same diet for two weeks, with 8.5 or 5.5 hours in bed. On short sleep, people lost less than half as much fat, lost more lean mass, and were hungrier.

Who was studied

None of these are lifters in a deficit. Kohanmoo pooled healthy adults. Ello-Martin studied women with obesity. Hall studied 20 weight-stable adults for two weeks each way, not dieters. Nedeltcheva had only 10 people. Each one is small or indirect on its own. Together they point in the same direction, and none of the advice carries any risk.

What to do

  • Protein at every meal: about 0.4 g/kg (more here).
  • Half the plate vegetables or fruit. More food on the plate for the same calories.
  • Build meals from whole foods. Ultra-processed snacks are the easiest calories to over-eat.
  • Protect 7–9 hours of sleep. Short sleep makes everything harder.
  • Starving all day? The deficit may be too big. Check your rate of loss, or plan a diet break.
