Overstated

Can you burn fat off one specific spot?

Short answer

Mostly no. Training an area makes the muscle stronger, but the fat you lose comes from all over your body, in an order set mostly by genetics and sex. A couple of trials have found a small local effect, most haven't. Some areas really are slower to lean out, and the fix is more total fat loss and more patience.

The answer depended on how it was measured. Skinfold calipers showed a local loss in the trained arm in men but not women. MRI, which sees the whole arm, showed fat loss spread evenly with no difference between arms.

Why some fat is slower

This part is about how it works in the body, not a training study. Fat cells carry two kinds of adrenaline receptor: one releases fat, one puts the brakes on. In lab studies of fat tissue from women, under-the-skin fat from the belly and thighs had more of the braking type than fat from around the organs, which came from a separate surgical group (Mauriège 1987). That's one likely reason the lower belly, hips and thighs are often the last to go.

Who was studied

Small groups, mostly untrained or overweight adults, measured with different tools (DEXA, MRI, calipers), and that alone changes the answer. The mechanism work is fat tissue in a dish. Taken together: any local effect is small and inconsistent, and nowhere near a strategy.

What to do

  • Train the area for strength and shape, not to burn its fat.
  • Focus on total fat loss: the deficit, protein and training.
  • Expect your last areas to be last. They're set by genes and sex, and they come off when overall body fat is low enough.
  • Measure the area (waist, hips, thigh) along with the scale, so you can see slow progress.
  • Skip creams, wraps and belts. None of them change fat.
